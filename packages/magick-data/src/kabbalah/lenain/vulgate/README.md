# The Vulgate's verses for the 72 genii

Each of Lenain's genii has a verse to recite: a psalm verse for seventy-one
of them, and for the seventieth, Jabamiah, "le 1er verset de la Genèse",
which his entry gives only in French. The edition's `psalm.la` is his Latin
as he prints it. This directory holds it to the Vulgate (plan 096, stage
Y0), since an angel's talisman letters the verse round its ring and a slip
there is a picture, not a line in a table.

- `verses.json5`: the Vulgate's text of each genius's verse, with Lenain's
  reference and the Vulgate's, and the page of a printed Clementine that
  has it. Generated by `scripts/seventyTwoAngels/vulgate.ts`; do not edit
  it by hand.
- `differences.json5`: every place Lenain's Latin differs from it, with
  what the 1823 printing and Kircher read and whose the difference is.
  Read by hand.
- `../../lenainVulgate.test.ts` holds every `psalm.la` to its verse. A
  difference not listed, or a listed one that no longer differs, fails.

The two files are built into `dist/` like the package's other data, but
not yet published: `package.json`'s `files` leaves this directory out
until the owner signs off their rows in the package README's Sources.

## Source

The text is the Clementine Vulgate Project's (VulSearch,
`vulsearch.sourceforge.net`), the Sixto-Clementine Vulgate of 1592
transcribed and proof-read by volunteers. Its two books are read from a
copy of the project's plain-text files, fixed at a commit:

The copy is the `latin/` directory of `jrichter/ClementineVulgateConverter`
on GitHub, commit `38292f3f91e874d3db9e7e9bf7abd23da3217054` (21 October
2015), whose README says the files were downloaded from the project's
site. The project's own site now redirects to its SourceForge page, and
its releases there (`clemtext/source`: an archive of 2005 and dated diffs
to 2 September 2006) are not the plain book files; no archived copy of
those files was found on 23 November 2026, when the Wayback Machine was
offline. Any copy with these bytes will do: the script checks each file's
size and SHA-256 and refuses any other bytes.

```bash
node --import tsx scripts/seventyTwoAngels/vulgate.ts <dir holding Ps.lat and Gn.lat>
```

## What we changed

- **Which verses.** Only the seventy-two: each genius's verse, two verses
  where Lenain's quotation runs across a verse division (21: Psalm
  30:15–16, 31: Psalm 70:15–16), and Genesis 1:1.
- **The markup.** The project marks lines of verse with `/`, passages set
  as verse with `[` and `]`, and paragraphs with `\`; these are taken out
  and the spaces closed up. Its spelling, capitals and punctuation,
  spaces before `: ; ? !` included, are kept.
- **The encoding.** Code page 1252 to UTF-8.

## Where each verse is

Lenain names each verse by psalm and verse. His psalms are numbered as the
Vulgate numbers them, but his verse numbers often are not: for 29 of the 71
the Vulgate's reference is another. It differs by one verse for 19 (12 one
more, where the Vulgate counts a psalm's title as its first verse, and 7
one fewer), by more for 7, and two quotations run across two verses (21 and
31). `verses.json5` gives both references; the data keeps Lenain's. Only
one psalm number differs, the thirty-third genius's
(`differences.json5`).

Each verse links its page in a printed Clementine: M. Hetzenauer's edition
(Innsbruck, 1914), archive.org
[`biblia_sacra_vulgatae_etitionis_1914`](https://archive.org/details/biblia_sacra_vulgatae_etitionis_1914),
marked with the Public Domain Mark (`printed.leaf` is the `n` of its page
URLs). The leaves were found by searching archive.org's OCR of the edition
and checked against the chapter and verse numbers in each page's running
head. Its spelling is not always the project's: it prints "exultate"
where the project prints "exsultate" (Psalm 97:4).

## The comparison

The test compares letters only: case, accents, ligatures written out or not
(`æ`, `ae`), `j` for `i`, punctuation and where words divide ("usque quo",
"usquequo") are ignored, since none of them is a reading. An apostrophe is
kept.

On 23 November 2026, after the fixes below, 58 of the 71 psalm verses
agreed with the Vulgate. The thirteen that do not are listed in
`differences.json5`: ten are Lenain's wording (one of them, the first,
partly Kircher's), and three are spellings of the period ("exultate",
"expectans"). Every one is in the 1823 printing as in the reissue.

The audit also found seven differences that were not Lenain's, and the
data now gives his reading:

- **Our transcription's**: genius 43's "prœveniet" and 54's "cœlo". The
  reissue prints "præveniet" and "cælo", with æ (pp. 75 and 83); the
  transcription had œ. Corrected in the transcription, the entries and the
  field alike.
- **The reissue's**: 2's stray mark in "a me'ad", 17's "Deus" for
  "Dominus", 37's added "ad", 47's "Psalm 104" for 103 and 64's
  "misericordiam" for "misericordia". The 1823 printing has the right
  reading in each. The entry's French keeps what the reissue prints; the
  field gives the 1823 reading, and a note in the apparatus says so
  (`../witnesses.json5`, `scripts/seventyTwoAngels/notes.json5`).

## Kircher, Lenain's source

Lenain says (p. 45, note 1) that Kircher gives the verses "en hébreu et en
latin", in *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* vol. II, p. 275. Kircher's table runs from
p. 275 to p. 280, a verse for each genius with its psalm and verse
(archive.org `bub_gb_XjfzMnomDQoC`, the Complutense copy, leaves n282 to
n287). Where it gives the same verse, `differences.json5` records Kircher's
Latin, which mostly agrees with the Vulgate, so the departures are
Lenain's. Lenain does not always take Kircher's verse: for the 24th, 47th,
50th and 63rd genii Kircher gives another. For the seventieth Kircher gives
Genesis 1:1 in Latin, "In principio creavit Deus cœlum & terram. Gen. 1."
(p. 280, leaf n287); the verse the site letters is the Vulgate's.
