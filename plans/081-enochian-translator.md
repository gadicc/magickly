# An Enochian translator by lookup — 13 November 2026

Status: designed with Gadi on 13 November 2026, decision by decision,
adversarially reviewed by Fable, spiked, and revised after the spike
(the same day). Gadi then confirmed the plan's proposals, adding
Leitch's spellings as input ("Confirmed with Gadi"), and said to
build it. Stage 1 was built on `gate/enochian-translator` in steps B
to G (13 and 14 November 2026); "Build notes" records what the build
settled and changed, and "Follow-ups" what is left. Read
[current status](000-current-status.md) first.

[Plan 077](077-enochian-translator-experiment.md) asked whether an LLM
with our data could translate Enochian. Its answer, and round 2's
(stopped before any call), shape this plan: with our data in the
prompt, a model reads Enochian into English well, but under "never
coin" English into Enochian is mostly a lookup problem. A model added
about 16 right words beyond strict lookup in 60 sentences, most of them
irregular verbs a small table gives, against about 35 stretched ones.

So the translator is built in two stages:

1. **Stage 1, lookup** (this plan, in full). A script, run in the
   reader's browser, in both directions. No model, nothing sent away,
   no running cost.
2. **Stage 2, an AI reading** for Enochian into English (outlined
   below, designed later). In plan 077 its gain over lookup was on
   joins (lookup 0 of the 40 new joins, Opus 40), but that lookup had
   no join rule; stage 1's rule splits all 40, by construction, since
   they were generated by the same small words. What an AI reading
   adds beyond the rule is unmeasured.

## What exists

- **The dictionary page** ([plan 074](074-enochian-dictionary-search.md)):
  one query searched both ways, the Enochian keypad, entries in a pane
  or a sheet, URL state, and `searchDictionary` in
  `src/app/enochian/dictionary/search.ts`, which returns groups so "a
  later translator adds a group without touching the ranking" (its
  decision 5). Its `spellings()` finds headwords containing the query,
  not near spellings.
- **The Keys page** and `src/app/enochian/keys/keyEntries.ts`, with
  `findParts` and `DO_NOT_SPLIT` for split words.
- **The data** (`packages/magick-data/src/enochian/`): `keys.json5`
  (Casaubon's words and Dee's English per row, Crowley's Golden Dawn
  readings, `englishSplit` where we split Dee's English), and
  `dictionary.json5` (1,911 headwords from Schueler, cited `EMPM`, and
  *The Whole Enochian Dictionary*, cited `WE`), and `grammar.md`, our
  40 grammar notes with sources.
- **`src/app/enochian/translate/`** holds plan 077's `bars.ts` and
  `limitations.ts`, written for an AI translator.
- **The chat's daily allowance** (`src/app/chat/usage.ts`): 100
  submissions a day per signed-in user, 500 shared by anonymous
  readers.

## Decisions

Settled with Gadi on 13 November 2026. Nine came first; the stage-1
review reworked several (seven more, and fixes adopted without a
decision), and the spike adjusted seven (decisions 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 15
and 21 here, each marked). They are numbered afresh here, each in its

### The product

1. **Lookup first, an AI reading later.** Stage 1 needs no model.
   Stage 2 adds an optional "Read with AI" for Enochian into English,
   after a cheaper model (Sonnet 5.5 or Haiku 5.5) is tested on plan
   077's tasks 1 and 2 (about $5).
2. **AI readings use the existing allowance.** Stage 2's readings draw
   on the reader's own daily allowance, or the anonymous group's
   (`reserveDailyChatUsage`), not a budget of their own. The counter
   counts submissions; a reading sends a much larger prompt than a
   chat turn, so stage 2 settles whether it counts as one or is
   weighted.
3. **Its own page, and a preview in the dictionary.** The tool is
   `/enochian/translate`. The dictionary shows an "as a sentence"
   preview for a query of three words or more, linking to the page.
   Both run the same code. (As built in step F: the preview is its own
   section above the list, not a group in `searchDictionary`'s
   results, since the lookup loads later and a listbox may hold only
   options; it shows the guessed reading only, in at most two lines,
   and English into Enochian carries a one-line form of decision 5's
   notice.)
4. **The direction is guessed, with a switch.** Both readings are
   scored: the share of words that are Enochian headwords, Key words,
   Leitch's spellings (decision 13) or words the join rule splits
   (decision 15) against the share that are
   English words. English wins a tie. The guess shows beside the
   box, "Reading as English → Enochian · switch". An input of one or
   two words that scores on both sides ("I", "fire", "a man") shows
   both readings. The preview uses the same guess.
5. **Both directions ship before an expert review** (overriding plan
   077's gate, which held "before a translator is shown to readers").
   English into Enochian shows a short notice
   above its results: no one who reads Enochian has reviewed it; it is
   reconstruction from Dee's words and our notes; a gap is a word we
   have no form for. The full limitations sit beside it. An expert's
   review stays on the list (see "For an expert").

### English into Enochian

The translator never coins a word and never builds a form Dee didn't
write. Each English word becomes one of three things: a **form**, from
the Keys or the dictionary; a **rule chip**, naming the grammar note
that leaves the word out or folds it; or a **gap**, with the nearest
forms where any exist.

6. **Phrases first.** The English is tokenised after contractions are
   expanded ("it's" to "it is", before `words()` strips "'s"), then
   matched in order, contiguous, longest phrase first (up to six
   words), Dee's line glosses before dictionary meanings. "over you"
   gives VORSG, "cast down" ADRPAN, "his power" LONSHITOX. A phrase
   must lie within one Key line's English to match a line gloss.
   No phrase crosses `.`, `!`, `?`, `;`, `:` or a possessive; commas
   and line breaks don't end one. Notes in brackets are dropped from
   Dee's English and the glosses, never from what the reader types,
   and a word in another script is a gap. The page and the preview
   read the first 500 words and say so.
7. **Exact forms, in tiers** (changed after the spike). A gloss
   matches when it equals the English, a word matching only itself or
   a regular inflection of it (-s, -es, -ies, -ied, -ed, -ing, -eth,
   -est), so "stare" doesn't find "stars" (step G). A letter's name in
   WE ("Pal (X)") is not a gloss. Tier 1 apart from articles
   (though a phrase begins or ends with "a", "an" or "the", or ends
   with a vocative "O", only where one of the matched glosses does, so "Open the gates" doesn't take the
   article into ODO "open"; an article left over takes its chip, and
   the phrase starts at the next word, so "the earth quakes" is
   GIZYAX; added in step D; and grammar words alone, such as "I am"
   ZIR, take a form only from a gloss that is exactly them, Dee's or the
   dictionary's (OROCH "under you", TILB "of her"), so "of his"
   doesn't find CNOQOD through WE's "servants, his"; added in step E);
   tier 2 apart from a leading "of" or "to"; tier 3 apart from one
   preposition, shown with a chip ("carries 'in'"). Be-forms,
   negation and pronouns are never ignored, so ZIRDO "I am" is not a
   form of "I". WE's inverted entries ("PEACE, IN") are read only as
   written forwards ("in peace"), so ETHARZI is a tier-3 form of
   "peace" (as the spike corrected it). A pronoun tail inverts too
   ("servants, his" is "his servants", never "his"), though the head
   stays a gloss of its own; a pronoun head is the meaning (TILB
   "HER, OF" is "of her"; added in step E).
8. **Choosing between forms** (changed after the spike). The best
   tier first. Among forms matched on Dee's own lines, the one he uses
   most for that sense, counting the closing formula of Keys 1 and
   11–18 (G40) once; on a tie, the earlier Key. Then dictionary
   matches: the shortest gloss, then the most matching meanings, then
   spelling (preferring Key forms among dictionary matches too, as
   first decided, picked O, a number in Key 11, over OI for "this").
   The word is marked when other forms exist; tapping it lists each
   with its source. Dictionary-only forms appear, labelled with their
   source.
9. **Grammar words follow our notes, each change shown** (changed
   after the spike).

   | English | Note | What the translator does |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | the, a, an | G4 | left out, chip |
   | of | G5 | left out, chip (DE is never chosen: the tool can't tell where Dee would write it; forms like OIAD "of God" still come through phrases) |
   | have, shall, do, will (auxiliaries) | G16 | left out, chip; right after an article or possessive the word is a noun ("thy will"), and before a wh-word, a determiner, another auxiliary or a clause's end a main verb ("Do what thou wilt"), looked up only by a gloss that is that word alone; "have" before a noun is looked up (BRIN) |
   | is, are, was, were, art, I am, am I | G13, G14 | Dee's forms (I, CHIS, ZIROP, ZIROM, GEH, ZIR); a lone "am" is ZIR, marked as carrying "I" |
   | a verb's tense or person | G16 | the verb's one form, by an irregular-verb table (came, kept, forgot, said, gave) |
   | not | G33 | IP, a separate word before the one it negates (GE, which follows its word by G32, is never chosen) |
   | no, none | G34 | AG |
   | to (infinitive), let, never, a lone be or been, modals (can, could, may, might, must, should, would) | | a gap, unless a phrase matches (G17's "let" forms come through phrases) |

   The auxiliaries include their old and inflected forms (did, does,
   doth, had, has, hast, hath, shalt, wilt). A superlative read as its
   base ("greatest" as "great") is marked a near match, with no rule.

10. **Pronouns** (changed after the spike). A pronoun with an exact
    form takes it (OL "I", TILB "her", TOX "his"). One without becomes
    a visible gap, not an "implied" chip: otherwise "he killed her" and
    "she killed him" come out the same. "Her" becomes TILB only before a
    content word ("her house") or standing alone; elsewhere it is an
    object and a gap.
    Phrases come first, so "her course" gives Dee's own ELZAPTILB.
11. **Possessives follow their noun, written apart** (changed after
    the spike). TOX and TILB come after the noun, as Dee mostly writes
    them (G22, which also has TILB once before its noun), but as
    separate words, never joined: SALMAN TILB, not SALMANTILB.
    "God's house" is reordered "house God" by G5, with a chip. Joining
    would build a form Dee didn't write.
12. **Gaps.** A gap shows the nearest forms, each with its source:
    [master: nearest GEIAD "our Lord and Master"]. One gap per content
    word, and nearest forms from `searchDictionary`'s "means" group
    and Key line glosses sharing the word's stem, three at most; none
    for a grammar word or a stem under three letters. Many gaps have
    none (as built, 37 of the 60 sentences' 226 gaps have any; 44 of
    227 before the fixes of 15 November, which left a preposition or
    conjunction only nearest forms of grammar words).

### Enochian into English

13. **Lookup from the Keys, then the dictionary.** Casaubon's words,
    Crowley's Golden Dawn readings (G38) and Leitch's spellings are
    accepted, the latter two mapped to Dee's word and labelled with
    their source; a Golden Dawn form spanning two of Dee's words
    carries both glosses. Leitch's spellings (vol. II's lexicon) are
    few. Of the forms in his lexicon with a Key reference, all but 34
    are Key words or headwords once the letters the search takes as
    one are folded. Each of the 34 was checked against the book's
    images on 13 November 2026 (the extract is OCR, and 6 other
    misreadings proved to be words we have): 26 are words he writes
    joined (ODZAMRAN, DSINSI, QTA, SOBAIAD), 6 differ in spelling
    (PRGEL for PURGEL, ZOMDUX, TOLHAM, FCAOSGA, VIVIALPRT, BAZEMLO),
    and 2 are other words at their place (BALTLE at his Key 1.70, TORB
    at his 10.41, by his numbering), left to the expert. The spellings
    and the joined forms go in a small table mapped to the Key rows
    they read, so to Dee's words, and cited by page; the join rule's
    splits don't matter for them. A spelling is a fact, and no gloss or
    other text of his is copied. The table lives in the app,
    `src/app/enochian/translate/leitchSpellings.ts`, not in
    `magick-data`, whose published data needs a signed-off Sources row
    (plan 080) and is CC BY 4.0.
14. **The reading line uses Dee's own English where it can.** Where
    the input matches a run of consecutive Key lines, the line shows
    those lines' English. Elsewhere it joins cleaned glosses: Dee's
    punctuation stripped, rows where we split Dee's English
    (`englishSplit`) shown with that English, marked as our division,
    and a short gloss defined for dictionary-only words (a first
    meaning is often a raw string). It is labelled "word by word".
    A run that begins or ends on a divided row still shows Dee's
    English unmarked: the words are his, and only where that row's
    English starts or stops is ours. The data records which rows we
    divided but not where Dee's own units begin, so such an edge can't
    yet be told from one he fixed; a follow-up in plan 000 is to
    record them. (Changed 14 November 2026: divided rows were to be blank on
    the line, but 60 Key words, SALMAN "the house" and OLPIRT "light"
    among them, have no other English.)
15. **Joined words are split by rule, and marked** (changed after the
    spike). Only a word not
    found whole. In front: G26's small words (DS, I, CHIS, TA, L, AR,
    OI, AG) and OD (G30), with I and L only before a Key word. Behind:
    TOX, TILB (G22). The rest a known word of three letters or more,
    allowing G27's shared letter. Two readings shown when two fit
    (ODSONF: OD SONF or OD DSONF). Each split is marked with the rule that made it ("split
    by rule G26"; G30 for OD, G22 for TOX and TILB, G27 for a shared
    letter). Q is not a prefix: in the spike it gave 2 wrong splits, no
    right ones, and a third of the false splits of typos.
16. **A word still not found** shows "not in our data" with the
    nearest spellings by edit distance (plan 074 has none): one edit
    for a word of four letters or fewer, two otherwise, five shown.

### The page

17. **Layout.** Going into Enochian: tiles (form over its English),
    rule chips and gaps. Going into English: the reading line, then
    tiles. A tile opens the word's entry, or its Key row for a word
    only in the Keys, as the dictionary does (a
    sheet on phones, the side pane on desktop). Splits, chips, gaps
    and tiers are told apart by text, not colour alone, and results
    are announced in a live region. Enochian input uses plan 074's
    keypad and right-to-left box.
18. **Rule chips open the rule.** A chip's popover shows the note's
    title and text from `grammar.md` (our own words, with its sources).
    No notes page is built in stage 1.
19. **Nothing leaves the browser.** The lookup runs client-side. The
    text goes in the URL fragment (`#q=…&dir=…`), never the query
    string, so it never reaches the server. The Keys
    table is slim and lazily loaded (as built, 12.5 KB gzipped; the
    page also loads the dictionary module the entries use, about 31 KB);
    the dictionary's preview loads the table and the lookup only for a
    query of three words or more (about 25 KB together; plan 074,
    decision 9, rejected 25 KB on that page's first load). The
    dictionary's own text is in its query string by plan 074's design,
    so its page sends only the origin as Referer.
20. **The limitations, per mode.** The lookup stage has its own list in
    `limitations.ts`, structured per mode so stage 2 adds its own
    (that the text goes to Anthropic). This plan quotes it word for
    word under a test, as plan 077 did; plan 077's list stays for its
    own record. The list Gadi accepted, with three items reworded to
    stay true under this design (the review found the first two too
    strong; the third changed when possessives moved after their noun),
    which Gadi confirmed on 13 November 2026:
    - This translator looks words up; it doesn't understand sentences.
      Each word is a form from Dee's Keys or our dictionary, labelled
      with its source, or a gap. Grammar words follow our notes' rules,
      and each change is shown.
    - Where our data has no exact word, you see a gap, with the nearest
      forms when we have any. It never makes up Enochian.
    - Word order follows the English, except that possessives follow
      their noun, as Dee writes them; each change is shown. No one who
      reads Enochian has checked the results.
    - Joined words are split by a rule from our notes. Each split is
      marked, and outside Dee's own text it may be wrong.
    - Meanings and rules come from Dee, Laycock and Leitch, and some
      from later traditions (Schueler, the Golden Dawn). Each is
      labelled with its source.
    - What you type isn't sent anywhere. The translation runs in your
      browser.
21. **Figures, script-only** (changed after the spike). The page
    shows two, computed from the shipped code by a committed script,
    written to a small numbers-only file beside `bars.ts`, which a
    test checks against a fresh run:
    - **Coverage on 60 sentences** (plan 077's task 3: practitioner,
      everyday and attested): the share of words given a form, a rule
      chip or a gap, and the gap-free sentences, by kind. A form that
      a rule gives (a form of "to be", a negation, a pronoun) counts as
      a rule chip, as in the spike, since each shows its chip. A form
      for one of Dee's phrases counts for each of its words, an
      article inside it included; a contraction counts as the words it
      stands for. This is the
      honest measure of new English, and the main figure. After the
      fixes of 15 November, of 622 words: forms 283 (46%), rule chips
      113 (18%; 41 of them forms a rule gives), gaps 226 (36%; 37 with
      a nearest form); gap-free 1 of 20 practitioner, 0 of 20
      everyday, 3 of 20 attested. (As built on 14 November: 280, 115
      and 227; the spike's: 51%, 11% and 38%.)
    - **Dee's English in, Dee's Enochian out**, each Key's English as
      running text: the share of his content rows whose word comes
      back. It is labelled a self-consistency check and has no margin
      of error, since the tool looks in the Keys themselves. 945 of
      1,054 (89.7%) after the fixes of 15 November; 946 as built, and
      the spike's 944.

    No margin of error on the coverage figure either, since the 60
    sentences are not a sample of what readers type. The
    date is the day the script last ran, not a test's.

## Stage 2: an AI reading and meaning hints

- "Read with AI" on Enochian into English: our dictionary, Keys and
  grammar notes as a cached prompt, plan 077's structured answer, the
  same checking script on the server before anything is shown.
- A cheaper model first passes plan 077's tasks 1 and 2 (done: GPT-6.1
  Terra, below).
- It draws on the existing allowance (decision 2), says where the text
  goes, and adds its limitations to the per-mode list.
- English into Enochian stays lookup. An option for stage 2's
  discussion (plan 077's recommendation, not decided): a model offering
  synonyms for a gap, marked as suggestions, never written into the
  text.

What stage 1 must leave for it: a pure lookup module that runs in the
browser and on the server alike, and the per-mode limitations.

### Decided with Gadi (15 November 2026)

- Reader text may leave the browser where that adds value (decision 19
  and the last limitation change with stage 2).
- Every AI Gateway call a reader triggers draws on their daily chat
  allowance, or the anonymous group's. A reading counts as one
  submission, plus one per 100 words; readings are capped at 150
  words (S3; first decided as 500, the lookup's cap). Site search keeps its own daily limit.
- Gap hints ("related: RNAH 'sunrise'") and related words on
  dictionary entries come from a table built once from embeddings, so
  readers trigger no AI call for them (the spike: the bare word's
  nearest glosses, shown above a cut-off, 95% good or related for
  about 30% of content gaps).
- Several cheaper models are tested before one is chosen (below).

- Terra's prompt is cached as the provider does by default (at least
  30 minutes for this model family, the review found); no longer
  retention and no keep-warm calls, so a reading after a quiet spell
  pays the cold price (16 November).
- "Read with AI" is offered only when the text goes beyond a run of
  Dee's own Key lines, whose English the lookup already shows.
- A server check confirms every Enochian form and meaning the model
  cites against our data; one that fails is shown, flagged as the
  model's guess, never silently and never withheld.
- An identical request is answered from a store for one day, costing
  no model call and no allowance, for anyone who sends the same text
  (shared, so a shared link's readers get it free; Gadi, 16 November,
  after first choosing a per-reader store for privacy). The
  limitations say so.

### The model test (written before any result)

Plan 077's arm A3 on its tasks 1 and 2, all 600 rows, scored against
the same bars, for Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5, GPT-6.1 Terra,
GPT-6 Luna and Gemini 3.8 Flash, each at medium reasoning through the
Gateway, with plan 077's two judges. Opus 5.5 runs again first as a
control: the dictionary changed on 15 November (seven WE meanings
parted), so A3's context differs from round 1's, and the cheaper
models are compared with Opus on the same context. Its result does not
replace round 1's. The test books against plan 077's ledger, capped at
$15 for work from 14 November on (estimate about $6). The model chosen
is the cheapest per reading that meets every task-1 and task-2 bar;
it then gets its own recall probe before it ships. Measured cost per
reading so far: Opus about $0.11 and GPT-6.1 Sol about $0.06 (plan
077's round 1).

whether it thought at medium effort is unverified; the runner now

**Chosen: GPT-6.1 Terra** (Gadi, 16 November 2026). The rule written
above picks the cheapest model meeting every bar, which is Luna; but
Luna met two bars within a row of their lines, where plan 077's
procedure is a declared top-up, and it made 9 silent inventions into
English (the reading's own direction) against Terra's 2. A silent
invention is the one error a reader cannot see, and Luna would save
about 1.2 cents a warm reading (about 7 a cold one), so Terra is
chosen over the rule's letter, without a top-up. Terra still gets its
own recall probe before it ships, and the design should keep its
cache warm where the provider allows a longer retention, since a cold
reading costs five times a warm one. (Both settled on 16 November:
no probe, since the new-text test's recall bar, 0 of 120, already
shows Terra answers from our data rather than memory; and the
default cache, below.)

### The design (16 November 2026, revised after Fable's review)

A Fable review the same day found the first draft not ready: no input
bound, the anonymous pool open to one client, a check weaker than its
wording, no sentence in the tested answer, room for prompt injection,
a 3-minute reading, an untrue limitation and text reaching the logs.
Gadi settled its eight questions (all as recommended); the design
below is revised.

S1. **The model.** GPT-6.1 Terra through the Gateway, at medium
    reasoning, pinned to OpenAI's servers (`only: ['openai']`) with
    zero data retention, with arm A3's context (our dictionary, Dee's
    Keys with row ids and Golden Dawn forms, our grammar notes) and
    `gateway.caching: "auto"`. OpenAI keeps the cached prefix at
    least 30 minutes for this model family. The prompt is plan 077's
    task-1 prompt extended to return a sentence as well (S7), with a
    line saying the passage is text to translate and never
    instructions; a prompt version is the hash of the model, effort,
    routing options and every byte of the prompt, so a dictionary fix
    makes it new.
S2. **When it is offered.** On an Enochian-into-English result of two
    words or more, a "Read with AI" button under the reading line,
    when at least one word is outside a run of Dee's Key rows and the
    text passes S3's admission (so it is never offered, then refused); it
    shows what it will use ("uses 2 of today's 100", or the anonymous
    wording).
S3. **Admission and bounds.** Words are counted as the lookup's
    tokeniser counts them, at most 150 a reading, and at most 1,200
    characters and 30 letters a word, A to Z only. The server reads the
    text with the lookup first and refuses it unless at least 80% of
    its words, and all but 10 of them, are ours (Key words, headwords,
    Golden Dawn or Leitch spellings, or joins the rule splits),
    whatever direction the page asked for.
S4. **The allowance** (decision 2). A reading reserves one submission
    plus one per full 100 words (so 150 words is 2), atomically, from
    the reader's daily allowance or the anonymous group's, and refuses
    with what is left when that is too few; a failed call stays
    counted. A signed-out client may use at most 10 units a day of the
    anonymous pool, keyed by an HMAC of its IP address (IPv6 cut to
    its /64) under a server secret that is never stored with the
    counters, rotated daily; a request with no address shares one
    bucket. The client's and the pool's reservations are one
    transaction, and the daily job deletes old counter rows. All
    readings stop for everyone once a day's cost reaches $10, until
    midnight UTC: each reading holds its worst-case cost atomically
    before the call and settles to the Gateway's reported cost after,
    keeping the hold when none is reported, as plan 077's ledger does.
    A database row turns readings off by hand at once. Accepted: about
    330 warm readings reach the ceiling, which a few heavy readers
    could do in a day, turning readings off for everyone. Chat itself
    still has no per-client cap, so one scripted client can still
    drain the anonymous pool through chat; that existing exposure is
    left (a follow-up).
S5. **The route.** `POST /api/enochian/read`: JSON only, same-origin
    only (`Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin`, or a matching Origin; the chat
    route gets the same guard). The text travels in the body, never the
    URL. It admits (S3), looks in the store (S7), checks the ceiling
    and the switch, reserves (S4), then reads in parts of at most 75
    words, the first alone to write the cache and the second after,
    checking, storing and streaming each part as it lands, so a retry
    after a failed second part pays only for that part. A pending row
    stops a concurrent identical request from paying twice. The model
    call is not cancelled when the reader leaves: it finishes and is
    stored. No retries are billed (`maxRetries: 0`), the function has
    an explicit `maxDuration` and an abort timeout, and the output cap
    per part comes from 2B's measured tokens per word (reasoning and
    the sentence included); a truncated answer counts as a failure.
    Every call asserts that the Gateway used zero data retention and
    OpenAI (unless 2B's outcome says otherwise). The grammar notes the
    context needs are generated into a module or traced into the
    function, not read from disk at run time.
S6. **The check.** Plan 077's checker, moved into the app and run on
    the server, extended: the lookup's accepted spellings (C/K, I/Y,
    U/V/W folds, Golden Dawn and Leitch forms) feed it; a derived
    item's source must exist and a join's parts must rebuild the word;
    and "confirmed" needs every content word of the model's English in
    the cited gloss, or, for a reading of grammar words only ("of
    the"), the same words as the gloss (plan 077's rule). Each input word gets exactly one row, in one of
    four states: confirmed by our data; the model's reading of a known
    word, not confirmed (the check couldn't settle it); the model's
    guess (a form or meaning we don't have); no reading. Only checked
    items reach the card, with the reader's own word and never the
    source text the model copied; a reading that isn't confirmed shows
    the model's English for that word, at most 10 words.
S7. **The card and the store.** The reading appears as a card under
    the lookup's reading line (option A of the mock-up): the model's
    sentence, labelled "the model's sentence, not checked", in plain
    text, at most a few times the input's length, with no links, and
    shown only when most of its content words appear in the word
    readings or the lookup's glosses (else withheld, saying so); then
    each word in its state with its source; then where the text went
    and what it used. States: loading part by part, the allowance or
    the ceiling reached (with the reset time), the provider refusing
    the passage, the model failing. Anyone who sends the same text
    within a day is answered from a shared store keyed by the prompt
    version and the exact text the prompt carries, at no cost or
    allowance (so a shared link's readers get it free; it also means
    anyone can tell whether a text was read that day, which the
    limitations say). "Read again" makes a new reading at the reader's
    cost and replaces the stored one. Rows older than a day are never
    served; each call deletes expired rows, and the daily job deletes
    any left, so a row lives under two days. Neon's point-in-time
    history may keep deleted rows for its window.
S8. **Its limitations** (decision 20's AI list; Gadi's wording of 16
    November; the second and third items revised after the reviews,
    the third settled with the shared store):
    - It is made by a language model (GPT-6.1 Terra) given our
      dictionary, Dee's Keys and our grammar notes; it can be wrong,
      and no one who reads Enochian has reviewed it.
    - Each word it reads is checked against our data and marked as
      confirmed, not confirmed, or the model's guess; its sentence is
      not checked.
    - Your text goes to OpenAI through Vercel to make the reading;
      Vercel keeps no copy, and OpenAI handles it under a
      zero-retention agreement. Our server keeps the text and its
      reading for a day, so anyone who reads the same text again gets
      it free, and deletes them within two days.
    - The provider may refuse some passages.
    - Each reading uses your daily allowance: one, plus one per 100
      words.
S9. **Decision 19 and the lookup's limitations.** The lookup still runs
    in the browser and its text stays in the fragment; its last
    limitation is reworded to say so of the lookup, and it gains one
    for the hints (machine-suggested from meanings, unreviewed). The
    stale "goes to Anthropic" in `limitations.ts` and here is fixed.
S10. **No text in logs.** Errors are caught and logged by class with
    counts, tokens, cost and latency only, never the text, the prompt
    or a hash of the text (the SDK's errors carry the whole request,
    as the chat route already guards); a test spies on the console
    during a failing call. The model id each response names is
    recorded, and the daily job compares the Gateway's listed price with
    the one the ceiling's holds assume, failing loudly when it drifts.
S11. **Gap hints and related words** (no reader-triggered call). A
    script embeds SCOWL's size-35 English words (about 50,000; words
    only, no names, abbreviations or contractions, and none the lookup
    already gives a form or folds as an inflection) and our glosses
    with the site's embedding model, bare words and the similarity
    recipe, once, run by Gadi (under $0.10). It prints the row count,
    bytes and 200 random hints to judge before it ships (it ships if at
    least 90% are good or related and at most 5% wrong, judged as the
    spike judged; else the cut-off rises until they are), keeps each
    word's best gloss above the spike's cut-off (z ≥ 5.5) within a
    budget of 150 KB gzipped, and records a hash of the glosses so a dictionary fix
    fails a test. The table is committed with SCOWL's notice and a
    hash-pinned source in a README, and AGENTS.md's generated-data rule
    is extended to name it (Gadi, 16 November). The page loads it
    lazily for a gap; a gap shows one hint, "related: RNAH 'sunrise'",
    never in the sentence. Each dictionary entry gets related words
    the same way (hub words removed, identical glosses merged).
S12. **Not in stage 2.** English into Enochian by a model; a
    part-of-speech tagger for "do" (a follow-up); root families as
    discoveries (an "other forms" list at most).

### 2C build notes (17 November 2026)

The checker is `src/app/enochian/translate/reading/check.ts`
(`checkReading`), pure and server-side, beside what moved there from
plan 077's scripts: `normalise.ts`, S7's `sentence.ts`, and the item
type and `parseRef` (`item.ts`). The experiment's scripts import them
from the app; plan 077's checker (`checkItem`) is unchanged, its tests
pass with only their imports changed, its prompts hash the same, and
2B's reading measures recompute to the saved `summary` and per-call
`rule`. The decisions S6 left open:

- **A word is the lookup's token.** One row for each, numbered from 1
  as the prompt will number them, so one of Crowley's spaced forms
  ("das iod") is one word. An item with no token or one out of range is
  dropped and counted, never shown; several on one word are a join.
- **Confirmed** needs basis "attested" or "derived" (a coinage claims
  no source, so it is never confirmed), a ref that resolves in our data,
  and its English in that source's gloss. One item is accepted for a
  word the lookup reads as one word (never one it reads only in parts:
  "das iod" is DSI and OD, so one item citing Key 4.40 reads half of
  it) when the lookup offers its row for that word, or its headword as
  the word's only entry, or when it is the word whole in one of our
  spellings: a fold, Crowley's whole form (never one of his spaced
  parts, so TA is not Key 4.32's "ta el-o"), or Leitch's spelling of one
  row. A Key word whose entries are its parts (TAGE opens TA and GE) is
  read in parts: GE's "not, only" is not TAGE's. A headword is found by
  the lookup's fold, so a ref with no dictionary entry (DSONF is only a
  Key word) doesn't resolve. A row's gloss is its English; a
  headword's, any one of its meanings. Key 19's slot is LIL's row, and
  every Aethyr read there opens LIL's entry: another Aethyr is accepted
  there only by the slot's row, checked against the lookup's gloss for
  it ("in the second air" for ARN) and shown as that Aethyr, never by
  LIL's headword.
- **English in a gloss.** Every content word of the model's English
  (stemmed, Dee's spellings folded, as plan 077 compares) is in one
  gloss. A reading of grammar words only needs the gloss's words, or a
  comma-separated piece's where the gloss itself is grammar words only:
  "I" meets "I, my", but "of the" does not meet CAOSGO's "earth, of
  the". A negation in the English (not, no, nor, never, none, neither,
  "-n't") needs the same word in the gloss, since plan 077's normalising
  takes "not" and "no" as grammar words ("not reign" would meet
  "reign"); plan 077's own scoring is unchanged.
- **Joins** are checked only as the lookup reads the word: one of its
  readings in parts (a split, G27's shared letter included, a form
  spanning rows, or a Key word's entries, TA + GE for TAGE), matched
  part by part, each part resolving and
  holding as above. Our spellings alone would rebuild nonsense from
  one-letter headwords (L + D + S), so there is no free concatenation:
  a known word whose parts aren't a reading of the lookup's is
  unconfirmed. A word with more than 8 items is not checked
  (unconfirmed, or the model's guess), its English still shown, cut.
- **Known** means the lookup has any reading of the word. A reading of
  one it hasn't is the model's guess, whatever its parts or refs.
- **Sources** appear only on confirmed and unconfirmed rows, for refs
  accepted for that very word (for a join, when its parts are the
  lookup's reading), as our data names them: the dictionary's key (of
  headwords that fold alike, the one the model wrote, else the first),
  or the Key and row with Dee's word there (in Key 19's slot, the
  Aethyr read). A guess or no reading has none.
- **English.** Each item's target is made plain text once, before any
  check: NFKC (fullwidth, mathematical and circled letters and
  ligatures become plain letters, so "ｎｏｔ reign" is "not reign"),
  controls and format characters removed. Every check and the row read
  that same text. A row's English is its items' plain targets joined,
  cut at 10 words (`cut`). "No reading" is no item, basis "unknown",
  English with no words or only "unknown", or English with a link (a
  scheme, "www.", an address with "@", or a dotted name ending in two
  letters or more; "e.g." is no link): such a row shows no English at
  all.
- **The sentence.** S7's evidence is the rows' English as shown plus the
  lookup's glosses (`tokenGlosses`, the passage read once). Beyond
  `sentenceRule`, the checker withholds a sentence with a link, or
  longer than 4 times the passage's words (at least 12): S7's "a few
  times", from 2B's 1.96 at most. Shown, it is plain text; withheld, the
  result has only the reason and S7's counts, no word of the model's
  (the eval's measures keep `sentenceRule`'s full verdict).
- **Cost.** The check is linear in the items and the passage: a passage
  S3 admits (150 words, 10 of them a letter off ours) with 20 items a
  word checks in well under 100 ms. The lookup's own reading dominates:
  150 near-miss words take it about 110 ms, which S3 never admits.

## The stage-1 review (Fable, 13 November 2026)

The review prototyped the design against the data and plan 074's
search. Its findings, and what became of them:

- **The exact-form rule picked wrong forms for common words.** With
  round 2's filler list, "I am" counted as "I", and Dee's ZIRDO "I am"
  (nine times, all in the closing formula) beat OL "I" (once); "her"
  gave TIOBL "in her", "house" ISALMAN "is a house". Fixed by tiers
  that never ignore be-forms, negation or pronouns (decision 7) and by
  counting the closing formula once (decision 8).
- **How the English is cut into phrases decided the result.** Word by
  word, Dee's English came back as his Enochian on 57% of content
  rows; longest phrase first, 85% (the spike: 89.6%). Fixed by
  decision 6.
- **The commonest gloss misled the reading line** on 50 of Dee's
  rows, and carried his punctuation. Fixed by decision 14.
- **"Implied" pronouns lost who did what.** Fixed by decision 10.
- **Three limitations were untrue as worded** (dictionary-only forms;
  few gaps get a nearest form; `?q=` reaches the server). The first
  two are reworded, and the text moved to the fragment (decisions 19,
  20).
- **The expert gate was dropped without a decision.** Gadi decided to
  ship before the review, with the notice (decision 5).
- **The planned figures were mostly true by construction** (joins and
  plants made by the same rules; Dee's rows looked up in Dee's rows)
  or rested on paid judges' verdicts a test can't refresh. Replaced by
  decision 21.
- **The join rule's 23 of 23 was a selection effect**: those were
  exactly the known joins. Held out, it split 30 Key words, 6 wrongly.
  I and L were narrowed, and after the spike Q was dropped (decision
  15).
- Also adopted: direction scored both ways (decision 4); edit-distance
  spellings (decision 16); a slim, lazy data table (decision 19);
  chips with somewhere to point (decision 18); sharing the spelling
  and split code with `keyEntries.ts`.

## Risks

- **English into Enochian is mostly gaps for everyday English** (56%
  of words in the everyday sentences as built; 59% in the spike). The notice and the
  figures say so; readers may still be disappointed.
- **Readers may take the output for Enochian an expert would write.**
  The notice, the labels and the chips are the only guard until the
  expert's review.
- **The join rule is wrong about one time in five** on Key words held
  out (5 of 25); each split is marked.
- **Dictionary-only meanings are uneven** (Schueler's, WE's; some
  angels' names and "(?)" glosses); each is labelled with its source.
- **Copyright.** No new text enters the repository: the Keys' English
  is Casaubon (1659) and Crowley (1912), public domain; the
  dictionary's text is already in the package and stays out of the
  published artefact (plan 052); the grammar notes are our own words;
  Leitch's spellings are facts, cited by page, in the app and not the
  published package.
