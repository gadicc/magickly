# An Enochian translator by lookup — 13 November 2026

Status: designed with Gadi on 13 November 2026, decision by decision,
adversarially reviewed by Fable, spiked, and revised after the spike
(the same day). A few details are this plan's proposals, listed under
"Proposed here, for Gadi". Not built: Gadi says when. Read
[current status](000-current-status.md) first.

[Plan 077](077-enochian-translator-experiment.md) asked whether an LLM
with our data could translate Enochian. Its answer, and round 2's
(stopped before any call), shape this plan: with our data in the
prompt, a model reads Enochian into English well, but under "never
coin" English into Enochian is mostly a lookup problem. A model added
about 16 right words beyond strict lookup in 60 sentences, most of them
irregular verbs a small table gives, against about 35 stretched ones.

So the translator is built in two stages:

1. **Stage 1, lookup** (this plan, in full). A script, run in the
   reader's browser, in both directions. No model, nothing sent away,
   no running cost.
2. **Stage 2, an AI reading** for Enochian into English (outlined
   below, designed later). In plan 077 its gain over lookup was on
   joins (lookup 0 of the 40 new joins, Opus 40), but that lookup had
   no join rule; stage 1's rule splits all 40, by construction, since
   they were generated by the same small words. What an AI reading
   adds beyond the rule is unmeasured.

## What exists

- **The dictionary page** ([plan 074](074-enochian-dictionary-search.md)):
  one query searched both ways, the Enochian keypad, entries in a pane
  or a sheet, URL state, and `searchDictionary` in
  `src/app/enochian/dictionary/search.ts`, which returns groups so "a
  later translator adds a group without touching the ranking" (its
  decision 5). Its `spellings()` finds headwords containing the query,
  not near spellings.
- **The Keys page** and `src/app/enochian/keys/keyEntries.ts`, with
  `findParts` and `DO_NOT_SPLIT` for split words.
- **The data** (`packages/magick-data/src/enochian/`): `keys.json5`
  (Casaubon's words and Dee's English per row, Crowley's Golden Dawn
  readings, `englishSplit` where we split Dee's English), and
  `dictionary.json5` (1,911 headwords from Schueler, cited `EMPM`, and
  *The Whole Enochian Dictionary*, cited `WE`), and `grammar.md`, our
  40 grammar notes with sources.
- **`src/app/enochian/translate/`** holds plan 077's `bars.ts` and
  `limitations.ts`, written for an AI translator.
- **The chat's daily allowance** (`src/app/chat/usage.ts`): 100
  submissions a day per signed-in user, 500 shared by anonymous
  readers.

## Decisions

Settled with Gadi on 13 November 2026. Nine came first; the stage-1
review reworked several (seven more, and fixes adopted without a
decision), and the spike adjusted seven (decisions 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 15
and 21 here, each marked). They are numbered afresh here, each in its

### The product

1. **Lookup first, an AI reading later.** Stage 1 needs no model.
   Stage 2 adds an optional "Read with AI" for Enochian into English,
   after a cheaper model (Sonnet 5.5 or Haiku 5.5) is tested on plan
   077's tasks 1 and 2 (about $5).
2. **AI readings use the existing allowance.** Stage 2's readings draw
   on the reader's own daily allowance, or the anonymous group's
   (`reserveDailyChatUsage`), not a budget of their own. The counter
   counts submissions; a reading sends a much larger prompt than a
   chat turn, so stage 2 settles whether it counts as one or is
   weighted.
3. **Its own page, and a preview in the dictionary.** The tool is
   `/enochian/translate`. The dictionary shows a one-line "as a
   sentence" preview for a query of several words (three or more is
   proposed here), linking to the page, as a new group in
   `searchDictionary`'s results. Both run the same code.
4. **The direction is guessed, with a switch.** Both readings are
   scored: the share of words that are Enochian headwords or Key words
   against the share that are English words. English wins a tie. The
   guess shows beside the box, "Reading as English → Enochian ·
   switch". An input of one or two words that scores on both sides
   ("I", "fire", "a man") shows both readings.
   The preview uses the same guess.
5. **Both directions ship before an expert review** (overriding plan
   077's gate, which held "before a translator is shown to readers").
   English into Enochian shows a short notice
   above its results: no one who reads Enochian has reviewed it; it is
   reconstruction from Dee's words and our notes; a gap is a word we
   have no form for. The full limitations sit beside it. An expert's
   review stays on the list (see "For an expert").

### English into Enochian

The translator never coins a word and never builds a form Dee didn't
write. Each English word becomes one of three things: a **form**, from
the Keys or the dictionary; a **rule chip**, naming the grammar note
that leaves the word out or folds it; or a **gap**, with the nearest
forms where any exist.

6. **Phrases first.** The English is tokenised after contractions are
   expanded ("it's" to "it is", before `words()` strips "'s"), then
   matched in order, contiguous, longest phrase first (up to six
   words), Dee's line glosses before dictionary meanings. "over you"
   gives VORSG, "cast down" ADRPAN, "his power" LONSHITOX. A phrase
   must lie within one Key line's English to match a line gloss.
7. **Exact forms, in tiers** (changed after the spike). A gloss
   matches when it equals the English: tier 1 apart from articles;
   tier 2 apart from a leading "of" or "to"; tier 3 apart from one
   preposition, shown with a chip ("carries 'in'"). Be-forms,
   negation and pronouns are never ignored, so ZIRDO "I am" is not a
   form of "I". WE's inverted entries ("PEACE, IN") are read only as
   written forwards ("in peace"), so ETHARZI is a tier-3 form of
   "peace" (as the spike corrected it).
8. **Choosing between forms** (changed after the spike). The best
   tier first. Among forms matched on Dee's own lines, the one he uses
   most for that sense, counting the closing formula of Keys 1 and
   11–18 (G40) once; on a tie, the earlier Key. Then dictionary
   matches: the shortest gloss, then the most matching meanings, then
   spelling (preferring Key forms among dictionary matches too, as
   first decided, picked O, a number in Key 11, over OI for "this").
   The word is marked when other forms exist; tapping it lists each
   with its source. Dictionary-only forms appear, labelled with their
   source.
9. **Grammar words follow our notes, each change shown** (changed
   after the spike).

   | English | Note | What the translator does |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | the, a, an | G4 | left out, chip |
   | of | G5 | left out, chip (DE is never chosen: the tool can't tell where Dee would write it; forms like OIAD "of God" still come through phrases) |
   | have, shall, do, will (auxiliaries) | G16 | left out, chip |
   | is, are, was, I am | G13, G14 | Dee's forms (I, CHIS, ZIROP, ZIR) |
   | a verb's tense or person | G16 | the verb's one form, by an irregular-verb table (came, kept, forgot, said, gave) |
   | not | G33 | IP, a separate word before the one it negates (GE, which follows its word by G32, is never chosen) |
   | no, none | G34 | AG |
   | to (infinitive), let, never, a lone be or been, modals (can, could, may, must) | | a gap, unless a phrase matches (G17's "let" forms come through phrases) |

10. **Pronouns** (changed after the spike). A pronoun with an exact
    form takes it (OL "I", TILB "her", TOX "his"). One without becomes
    a visible gap, not an "implied" chip: otherwise "he killed her" and
    "she killed him" come out the same. "Her" becomes TILB only before a
    content word ("her house"); elsewhere it is an object and a gap.
    Phrases come first, so "her course" gives Dee's own ELZAPTILB.
11. **Possessives follow their noun, written apart** (changed after
    the spike). TOX and TILB come after the noun, as Dee mostly writes
    them (G22, which also has TILB once before its noun), but as
    separate words, never joined: SALMAN TILB, not SALMANTILB.
    "God's house" is reordered "house God" by G5, with a chip. Joining
    would build a form Dee didn't write.
12. **Gaps.** A gap shows the nearest forms, each with its source:
    [master: nearest GEIAD "our Lord and Master"]. Proposed here: one
    gap per content word, and nearest forms from `searchDictionary`'s
    "means" group and Key line glosses sharing the word's stem. Many
    gaps have none (in the spike, 50 of 236 had any).

### Enochian into English

13. **Lookup from the Keys, then the dictionary.** Casaubon's words
    and (proposed here) Crowley's Golden Dawn readings (G38) are both
    accepted, the
    latter mapped to Dee's word; a Golden Dawn form spanning two of
    Dee's words carries both glosses.
14. **The reading line uses Dee's own English where it can.** Where
    the input matches a run of consecutive Key lines, the line shows
    those lines' English. Elsewhere it joins cleaned glosses: Dee's
    punctuation stripped, rows where we split Dee's English
    (`englishSplit`) shown as tiles only, and a short gloss defined for
    dictionary-only words (a first meaning is often a raw string).
    It is labelled "word by word".
15. **Joined words are split by rule, and marked** (changed after the
    spike). Only a word not
    found whole. In front: G26's small words (DS, I, CHIS, TA, L, AR,
    OI, AG) and OD (G30), with I and L only before a Key word. Behind:
    TOX, TILB (G22). The rest a known word of three letters or more,
    allowing G27's shared letter. Two readings shown when two fit
    (OISALMAN). Each split is marked with the rule that made it ("split
    by rule G26"; G30 for OD, G22 for TOX and TILB, G27 for a shared
    letter). Q is not a prefix: in the spike it gave 2 wrong splits, no
    right ones, and a third of the false splits of typos.
16. **A word still not found** shows "not in our data" with the
    nearest spellings by edit distance (plan 074 has none).

### The page

17. **Layout.** Going into Enochian: tiles (form over its English),
    rule chips and gaps. Going into English: the reading line, then
    tiles. A tile opens the word's entry, or its Key row for a word
    only in the Keys, as the dictionary does (a
    sheet on phones, the side pane on desktop). Splits, chips, gaps
    and tiers are told apart by text, not colour alone, and results
    are announced in a live region. Enochian input uses plan 074's
    keypad and right-to-left box.
18. **Rule chips open the rule.** A chip's popover shows the note's
    title and text from `grammar.md` (our own words, with its sources).
    No notes page is built in stage 1.
19. **Nothing leaves the browser.** The lookup runs client-side. The
    text goes in the URL fragment (`#q=…&dir=…`), never the query
    string, so it never reaches the server. The Keys
    table is slim and lazily loaded (about 15 KB gzipped, with the
    dictionary's meanings about 37 KB in all); the dictionary's
    preview loads it only for a query of three words or more (plan
    074, decision 9, rejected 25 KB on that page).
20. **The limitations, per mode.** The lookup stage has its own list in
    `limitations.ts`, structured per mode so stage 2 adds its own
    (that the text goes to Anthropic). This plan quotes it word for
    word under a test, as plan 077 did; plan 077's list stays for its
    own record. The list Gadi accepted, with three items reworded to
    stay true under this design (the review found the first two too
    strong; the third changed when possessives moved after their noun);
    the rewordings are for Gadi to confirm:
    - This translator looks words up; it doesn't understand sentences.
      Each word is a form from Dee's Keys or our dictionary, labelled
      with its source, or a gap. Grammar words follow our notes' rules,
      and each change is shown.
    - Where our data has no exact word, you see a gap, with the nearest
      forms when we have any. It never makes up Enochian.
    - Word order follows the English, except that possessives follow
      their noun, as Dee writes them; each change is shown. No one who
      reads Enochian has checked the results.
    - Joined words are split by a rule from our notes. Each split is
      marked, and outside Dee's own text it may be wrong.
    - Meanings and rules come from Dee, Laycock and Leitch, and some
      from later traditions (Schueler, the Golden Dawn). Each is
      labelled with its source.
    - What you type isn't sent anywhere. The translation runs in your
      browser.
21. **Figures, script-only** (changed after the spike). The page
    shows two, computed from the shipped code by a committed script,
    written to a small numbers-only file beside `bars.ts`, which a
    test checks against a fresh run:
    - **Coverage on 60 sentences** (plan 077's task 3: practitioner,
      everyday and attested): the share of words given a form, a rule
      chip or a gap, and the gap-free sentences, by kind. This is the
      honest measure of new English, and the main figure. The
      spike's, under the final rules: forms 51%, chips 11%, gaps 38%;
      gap-free 1 of 20 practitioner, 0 of 20 everyday, 3 of 20
      attested.
    - **Dee's English in, Dee's Enochian out**, each Key's English as
      running text: the share of his content rows whose word comes
      back. It is labelled a self-consistency check and has no margin
      of error, since the tool looks in the Keys themselves. The
      spike's: 944 of 1,054 (89.6%).

    Proposed here: no margin of error on the coverage figure either,
    since the 60 sentences are not a sample of what readers type. The
    date is the commit's, not a test's.

## Stage 2: an AI reading (outline, designed later)

- "Read with AI" on Enochian into English: our dictionary, Keys and
  grammar notes as a cached prompt, plan 077's structured answer, the
  same checking script on the server before anything is shown.
- A cheaper model first passes plan 077's tasks 1 and 2, after its own
  recall probe (about $5).
- It draws on the existing allowance (decision 2), says that the text
  goes to Anthropic, and adds its limitations to the per-mode list.
- English into Enochian stays lookup. An option for stage 2's
  discussion (plan 077's recommendation, not decided): a model offering
  synonyms for a gap, marked as suggestions, never written into the
  text.

What stage 1 must leave for it: a pure lookup module that runs in the
browser and on the server alike, and the per-mode limitations.

## The stage-1 review (Fable, 13 November 2026)

The review prototyped the design against the data and plan 074's
search. Its findings, and what became of them:

- **The exact-form rule picked wrong forms for common words.** With
  round 2's filler list, "I am" counted as "I", and Dee's ZIRDO "I am"
  (nine times, all in the closing formula) beat OL "I" (once); "her"
  gave TIOBL "in her", "house" ISALMAN "is a house". Fixed by tiers
  that never ignore be-forms, negation or pronouns (decision 7) and by
  counting the closing formula once (decision 8).
- **How the English is cut into phrases decided the result.** Word by
  word, Dee's English came back as his Enochian on 57% of content
  rows; longest phrase first, 85% (the spike: 89.6%). Fixed by
  decision 6.
- **The commonest gloss misled the reading line** on 50 of Dee's
  rows, and carried his punctuation. Fixed by decision 14.
- **"Implied" pronouns lost who did what.** Fixed by decision 10.
- **Three limitations were untrue as worded** (dictionary-only forms;
  few gaps get a nearest form; `?q=` reaches the server). The first
  two are reworded, and the text moved to the fragment (decisions 19,
  20).
- **The expert gate was dropped without a decision.** Gadi decided to
  ship before the review, with the notice (decision 5).
- **The planned figures were mostly true by construction** (joins and
  plants made by the same rules; Dee's rows looked up in Dee's rows)
  or rested on paid judges' verdicts a test can't refresh. Replaced by
  decision 21.
- **The join rule's 23 of 23 was a selection effect**: those were
  exactly the known joins. Held out, it split 30 Key words, 6 wrongly.
  I and L were narrowed, and after the spike Q was dropped (decision
  15).
- Also adopted: direction scored both ways (decision 4); edit-distance
  spellings (decision 16); a slim, lazy data table (decision 19);
  chips with somewhere to point (decision 18); sharing the spelling
  and split code with `keyEntries.ts`.

## Proposed here, for Gadi

## Steps

Each step is one gated commit on a `gate/` branch, when Gadi says to
build. (Steps are lettered so they aren't confused with stages 1 and
2.)

B. **Data fixes** (decision 22).

## Risks

- **English into Enochian is mostly gaps for everyday English** (59%
  of words in the spike's everyday sentences). The notice and the
  figures say so; readers may still be disappointed.
- **Readers may take the output for Enochian an expert would write.**
  The notice, the labels and the chips are the only guard until the
  expert's review.
- **The join rule is wrong about one time in five** on Key words held
  out (5 of 25); each split is marked.
- **Dictionary-only meanings are uneven** (Schueler's, WE's; some
  angels' names and "(?)" glosses); each is labelled with its source.
- **Copyright.** No new text enters the repository: the Keys' English
  is Casaubon (1659) and Crowley (1912), public domain; the
  dictionary's text is already in the package and stays out of the
  published artefact (plan 052); the grammar notes are our own words.
