# Regardie's *The Golden Dawn*: copyright status — 19 November 2026

Status: researched 19 November 2026 from the US Copyright Office's own
records; a records search, not legal advice. It answers the question plan
058 left for the operator (question 1), the "not established" of plan 061
and the "unverified" of plan 088. AGENTS.md ("Outside sources") carries
the rule that follows from it; cite this plan wherever a Regardie volume's
status matters.

## Why

Much of the site draws on Israel Regardie's *The Golden Dawn*: the temple
halls (vol. 2), the lamens (vols. 1–3), the fylfot cross and the Tree's
proportions (vol. 1), the Enochian tablets, the Watchtowers' sigils and
the geomantic Rulers (vol. 4), and the rose sigil's rules (vol. 4,
pp. 57–60). Each plan asked anew whether the book is public domain; this
records the answer once.

## The editions

The first edition is four volumes, Aries Press, Chicago, 1937–1940, the
printings archive.org holds: `IsraelRegardie-thegoldenDawn-vol1-1937`,
`IsraelRegardie-TheGoldenDawn-Vol2-1938`,
`IsraelRegardie-TheGoldenDawn-Vol3-1939` and
`IsraelRegardie-TheGoldenDawn-Vol4-1940`. Later editions (revised and
enlarged in 1969 and 1970, then Llewellyn's from 1971 to the 2015 edition
with John Michael Greer) add new matter that is separately protected;
nothing here applies to them.

## In the United States

A US book of 1937–1940 was protected for 28 years and then only if its
copyright was renewed in the 28th year (1909 Act). Each volume was
registered on its own:

| Volume | Registered | Renewed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (1937) | A108987, 20 July 1937 | **Yes:** R341660, 23 July 1964, claimant Israel Regardie. In US copyright to the end of 2032 |
| 2 (1938) | © 27 November 1938, Regardie | None found (due 1965–66). Public domain since 1967 |
| 3 (1939) | © 10 May 1939, Regardie | None found (due 1966–67). Public domain since 1968 |
| 4 (1940) | A141600, 27 May 1940, claimant the Aries Press | None found (due 1967–68). Public domain since 1969 |

The registrations are in the *Catalog of Copyright Entries*, Part 1,
Books, for 1939 (archive.org `catalogofcopyri361lib`, vols. 2 and 3) and
1940 (`catalogofcopyri371lib`, vol. 4). The renewals were searched for
"Regardie", "Golden Dawn" and "Aries Press" in two forms: NYPL's
transcription of every renewal (github.com/NYPL/cce-renewals,
`data/1963-1.tsv` to `data/1977-1.tsv`), and the scanned renewal sections
of the *Catalog* for 1965 to 1968 (`catalogofcopy19653191lib`,
`catalogofcopy19653191libr`, `catalogofco1966320112lib`,
`catalogofco1966320112libr`, `catalogofco1967321112lib`,
`catalogofco1967321112libr`, `catalogofco1968322112lib`,
`catalogofco1968322112libr`). Both find volume 1's renewal (the 1964 scan,
`catalogofcopyrig31812libr`, prints it), so the search finds renewals that
exist; neither finds one for volumes 2–4. The only other hits are a firm of
accountants, Regardie & Brooks, and an Easter cantata of 1946 called *The
Golden Dawn*. Regardie also renewed *My Rosicrucian Adventure* (1936) in
1964, and none of his books of 1938–1940.

Not checked: Stanford's Copyright Renewal Database, which put a
verification challenge in front of an automated search. It is built from
the same *Catalog*.

## Elsewhere

Regardie died in 1985. Where copyright lasts for the author's life and 70
years (the United Kingdom, the European Union and many others), all four
volumes are protected to the end of 2055; where it lasts for life and 50
years, to the end of 2035. Whether the rule of the shorter term (the EU
Term Directive, article 7; the UK's Copyright, Designs and Patents Act,
s. 12(6)) ends volumes 2–4 there as it did in the US depends on
Regardie's nationality (born in London in 1907, in the United States from
1921) and is not settled here.

Much of the book is the Order's own papers, by Mathers, Westcott and
others who died before 1930, first printed by Regardie. What his printing
adds that is protected, and how a first publication in 1937–1940 of older
manuscripts is treated abroad, is also not settled here.

## What follows for the site

The site is read worldwide, so every volume is treated as in copyright:

- **Facts** from any volume (rules, layouts, colours, correspondences,
  which letter goes where) are taken freely and drawn with our own art,
  as before.
- **Links** to its pages on archive.org are fine, as the sigil page and
  the components' sources already do.
- **Nothing is copied:** no quotation beyond a short phrase, no mirrored
  page (`src/sources/mirroredPages.ts` takes only works out of copyright
  everywhere), no traced figure. Should Gadi ever relax that to US public
  domain, it would cover the first edition's volumes 2–4 only, and
  volume 1 not before 2033.
